08Science

Melting diamond under extreme pressure settles a 20-year dispute and points to triple fusion gain

Physicists at Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory, publishing in Nature Physics, compressed diamond to roughly three times the pressure at Earth's core using the Omega Laser Facility and took X-ray measurements of its atomic structure during the billionth-of-a-second window. The data closed a long-standing 20% gap between measured and simulated melting temperatures. Because diamond forms the capsule holding fusion fuel in inertial confinement experiments, the team calculates that slower shocks could triple energy gain if other losses are controlled, and the same physics explains diamond rain inside Neptune and Uranus.
