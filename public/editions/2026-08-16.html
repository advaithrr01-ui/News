Israeli strikes kill 11 in southern Lebanon in deadliest exchange since June

An overnight strike on Ansar near Nabatieh killed seven people including three children, and Israeli aircraft hit Deir al-Zahrani hours later, killing at least four and injuring 17, the Lebanese health ministry said. Israel said the raids targeted Hezbollah infrastructure and followed a Hezbollah attack that seriously wounded three Israeli soldiers, later acknowledging civilian deaths and saying the women and children were family members of a targeted commander. These were the deadliest attacks since the June arrangements that eased hostilities along the border, and Hezbollah has threatened to retaliate.

04Science

DR Congo Ebola outbreak passes 2,180 deaths and spreads to a sixth province

The Democratic Republic of the Congo had recorded 4,665 confirmed cases and 2,184 deaths as of 13 August, an increase of 99 cases and 56 deaths in a single day. Africa CDC said the outbreak had reached Bas-Uele after an imported case from Haut-Uele was identified in Buta, taking the total to 54 affected health zones across six provinces. Caused by Bundibugyo ebolavirus, for which existing Ebola therapeutics are not certified, it is now the fastest-growing Ebola outbreak on record and the second largest.

Baguio recorded 1,413.2mm of rain between 1 and 9 August against a typical August total of 963.2mm. More than half a million people across 33 provinces have been affected, with 182 areas in Luzon flooded.

09Science

Perseverance films Earth vanishing behind Phobos as Webb spots an early-universe oddity

NASA's Perseverance rover captured Earth disappearing behind Mars's small moon Phobos on 13 August, a first for the mission. The same week, astronomers using the James Webb Space Telescope reported an unusual object dating to just a few hundred million years after the Big Bang. A separate study published on 15 August warned that measurements of exoplanet rotation may often be recording atmospheric wind speeds instead, noting that Venus's clouds circle the planet about 60 times faster than Venus itself rotates.

02Policy

India sets a 100 GW nuclear target for 2047 with five reactors due within six to seven years

Modi used the Red Fort address to commit India to 100 GW of nuclear capacity by 2047, citing the SHANTI Act passed by Parliament that opens the sector to private participation. He said five new reactors would be commissioned within six to seven years and pointed to this year's fast breeder reactor milestone as progress towards domestic fuel self-reliance. The speech also flagged an offshore exploration push and framed critical minerals, nuclear and renewable power as the energy base for AI, semiconductors and data centres.
