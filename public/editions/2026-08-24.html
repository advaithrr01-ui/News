08Science

China's Chang'e-7 sets off for the lunar south pole to hunt for water ice

A Long March 5 lifted off from Wenchang on Hainan with a launch window opening at 0000 UTC on 24 August, carrying an orbiter, a lander, a rover and a hopping robot designed to leap into permanently shadowed craters. The target is the rim of Shackleton Crater, a 21km pit grazing the pole, and it would be the first landing directly at the lunar south pole. 09Science

Hidden small stars make the universe's earliest galaxies up to four times heavier than thought

An international team combined JWST observations of nine massive, long-dead galaxies with earlier Very Large Telescope data and found a far larger population of faint low-mass stars than models assumed. The revision makes some of those galaxies three to four times more massive, deepening the puzzle of how such large systems assembled so soon after the Big Bang. The work, published in Nature Astronomy, also implies planets around low-mass stars may have been more common in the early universe.

08Science

IIT Madras finds the ocean's rarest bacteria are what hold microbial communities together

Researchers at the institute's Wadhwani School of Data Science and AI analysed more than 4,600 publicly available ocean samples spanning tropical, temperate and polar waters. Using network analysis they found that rare bacteria, despite tiny population shares, act as connectors linking otherwise separate microbial groups. Losing those bridges could destabilise marine microbial communities in ways current models cannot predict. The work appears in the journal mSystems.
