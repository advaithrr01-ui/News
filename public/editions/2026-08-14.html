08Science

Ingredients for rocky planets may have existed 100 million years after the Big Bang

University of Portsmouth researchers argue that Population III supernovae, the deaths of the universe's first and largest stars, scattered carbon, oxygen and iron into surrounding gas far earlier than models assumed. That would place the raw material for rocky worlds, and possibly water, in circulation before the first galaxies had formed. The finding pushes the accepted start of planet building back by billions of years.
